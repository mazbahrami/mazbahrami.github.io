Biography

Maziyar Bahrami is an interdisciplinary researcher whose work connects Information Systems, management and organization studies, artificial intelligence, and data science. He studies how emerging technologies are designed, governed, adopted, and used within organizations and wider social systems.

His academic background spans mechanical engineering, finance, business administration, data science, and management. This path has encouraged him to examine technological phenomena from organizational, computational, and human perspectives rather than from a single disciplinary lens. His principal research interests include AI governance, organizational AI adoption, governance by design, cybersecurity management, digital transformation, and collaborative and privacy-preserving AI.

His broader intellectual interests include cognitive science and computational social science, particularly questions concerning human judgment, organizational behaviour, technology use, and the analysis of social and behavioural data. These interests complement rather than replace his central work in Information Systems and management.

Before focusing primarily on academic research, Maziyar accumulated several years of professional experience in business analysis, market research, sales development, sales data analysis, and research-oriented roles. He worked with large organizations and interdisciplinary teams, using data and analytical reasoning to address commercial and organizational problems. This experience continues to shape his research by connecting theoretical questions with practical realities.

He is currently pursuing a PhD in Management at Luiss University under the supervision of Professor Paolo Spagnoletti.

How I work

I am most interested in problems that cannot be understood through a single method or discipline. Depending on the question, my work may combine conceptual development, Design Science Research, computational modelling, machine learning, natural language processing, network analysis, causal reasoning, simulation, or traditional quantitative analysis.

My aim is not to apply technology for its own sake, but to understand how technical design, organizational arrangements, governance choices, and human behaviour shape one another.
