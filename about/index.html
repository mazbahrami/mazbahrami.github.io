Background

Different disciplines, one research direction

AI governance is not only a policy problem. It also depends on organizational decision-making, technical architecture, evidence, incentives, and the ability to intervene when conditions change. My background developed across these boundaries before they became the focus of my research.

Engineering gave me a foundation in systems thinking, modeling, simulation, and validation. Finance, consulting, and digital commerce brought attention to incentives, risk, organizations, and decision-making. Data science strengthened my empirical and computational work. Blockchain and federated learning gave me practical exposure to traceability, permissions, verification, and distributed technical architectures.

Information Systems now provides the field in which these experiences come together. Rather than treating them as separate research interests, I use them to study a more focused problem: how AI and digital systems can be governed in ways that are operational, adaptive, and technically credible.

What I Bring to the Research

Technical and organizational grounding

The value of my interdisciplinary background is not breadth for its own sake. It gives me several ways to understand the same governance problem.

01

Systems and Engineering

Computational modeling, simulation, model assumptions, validation, and systems thinking. This background shapes how I think about control, feedback, failure, and the relation between components and system-level outcomes.

02

Business and Organizations

Finance, consulting, commercial analysis, forecasting, market research, and decision support. These experiences help me study governance as an organizational problem involving authority, incentives, uncertainty, coordination, and accountability.

03

Data, AI, and Computation

Statistical analysis, machine learning, NLP, causal methods, programming, and large-scale data work. I use computational methods when they make organizational or governance dynamics more measurable and testable.

04

Distributed Systems and Verification

Blockchain development, federated learning, smart contracts, traceability, permissions, and privacy-preserving verification. This provides a technical basis for research on governance by design and assurance mechanisms.
