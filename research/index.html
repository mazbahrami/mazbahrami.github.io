Research

Understanding—and designing—the governance of emerging technologies.

My research connects organizational theory, Information Systems, system design, and computational evaluation to investigate responsible technology in practice.

Research agenda

Three connected levels

Organizations define needs and constraints; governance translates them into requirements; design and evaluation test whether those requirements can operate in practice.

01

AI governance and organizations

I investigate how organizations adopt, govern, monitor, and remain accountable for AI systems. This includes governance structures, lifecycle controls, organizational capabilities, risk ownership, and the relationship between formal policy and operational practice.

  • Organizational AI adoption and capability development
  • Accountability and lifecycle governance
  • Institutional and regulatory pressures
  • Trustworthy AI in organizational settings
02

Governance by design

Design-oriented research can move governance beyond principles and checklists by embedding selected requirements into the architecture and operation of digital systems. My work explores traceability, permission, organizational attribution, privacy-preserving verification, and controlled participation.

  • Design Science Research
  • Design requirements and design principles
  • Governance mechanisms in digital infrastructures
  • Evaluation of socio-technical artifacts
03

Computational and interdisciplinary inquiry

I use computational methods when they are appropriate to the research question—not as an end in themselves. My interests include machine learning, NLP, network analysis, simulation, computational modelling, causal methods, and data-driven analysis of organizational and social phenomena.

  • Machine learning and natural language processing
  • Network and ecosystem analysis
  • Computational social analysis
  • Advanced quantitative and causal methods

Conceptual map

From organizational problems to evaluated design.

The map presents a recurring logic in my work: contextual problems generate governance requirements, those requirements inform system design, and evaluation produces evidence about utility and limitations.
